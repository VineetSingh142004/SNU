Delta Theta began at Lombard College in Galesburg in 1867 and entered Sigma Nu in 1891. When Lombard closed in 1930, the brothers carried the charter across town to Knox — and the chapter has never gone dark since. Today it is the longest continuously running chapter at Knox College, and the oldest continuously running Sigma Nu chapter anywhere.

Some bonds outlast a lifetime

Brothers for four years · Knights for life

Born at VMI, forged in honor, carried west to the prairie, and kept alive for over a century

Sigma Nu was founded on January 1, 1869 at the Virginia Military Institute by James Frank Hopkins, Greenfield Quarles and James McIlvaine Riley — cadets who rejected the hazing of their day and built a Legion of Honor instead. Hopkins modelled the badge on the cross of the Légion d'honneur. At its heart, on black enamel, he set a golden coiled serpent.

Tenets

What makes a man a Knight of Sigma Nu? Not a legacy, not a letter — a standard. Six tenets define the Delta Theta brother, and every one of them is earned.

UnwaveringLove

Love is the first word of the Creed. It is the brother who shows up at 2 a.m., the hand on your shoulder before a final, the family you choose — and keep.

KnightlyHonor

Sigma Nu was founded on the rejection of hazing. Honor here is not inherited — it is practised daily, in how we treat each other, our campus and Galesburg.

RelentlessTruth

To serve in the light of truth: honest counsel, hard conversations, and a chapter that holds every man to the standard he pledged.

Every brother is a scholar first. Study hours, mentorship from upperclassmen and alumni, and a house that treats the library as home turf.

AcademicExcellence

SelflessService

From Galesburg food drives to campus clean-ups, the Legion serves the community that has hosted it for more than a century.

LifelongBrotherhood

The bond does not end at commencement. Delta Theta alumni span generations, careers and continents — and they still answer the call.
